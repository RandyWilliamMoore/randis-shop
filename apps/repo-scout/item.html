Do it

Clone makes a read-only copy (latest version only) in the folder you pick; Scout never runs anything from it. Fork and Watch open the project's page in your browser so you do the last click yourself, signed in as you.

What each choice means

  • Clone: copy the code to your computer to read it or try building on it. Nothing leaves your machine.
  • Use: install or run it as it is, beside Randis UDS. Best for big, active, well-kept tools.
  • Fork: make your own copy on GitHub to change it. Good when the original is abandoned or won't take your changes.
  • Integrate: bring the idea (or permissively licensed parts, with credit) into one of your tabs.
  • Watch: promising but not ready; star it and check back.
  • Study: read it for ideas only (also the right pick when there's no license).
  • Ignore: not useful for you.

Licenses in one breath

  • MIT / Apache / BSD / ISC / Zlib reuse freely; keep their credit line.
  • GPL / AGPL / LGPL / MPL fine to run beside your app; copying code into your app brings their rules with it.
  • no license look only: by default nobody may reuse it.

How Fit is scored

How many of your search words it matches (40), popularity by stars (25), recent activity (20), license friendliness (15). Archived projects lose points.

Resources for deciding

Good questions to ask before you pick

  • Was it changed in the last year? Are issues answered?
  • Is it one person's weekend project or used by many?
  • Does it run on Windows, offline, without accounts or keys?
  • Could a small tab of your own do the 20% you actually need?
  • If it's closed-source, is there an open version worth making?
